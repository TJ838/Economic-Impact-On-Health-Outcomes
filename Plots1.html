Visualizations for Distribution of Different Indicators

It is important to note that, the economic indicators such as GDP tend to have highly skewed distributions, meaning that a few extremely large values dominate the dataset. This creates problems when analyzing relationships between variables, as a small number of high-GDP countries can distort trends, making it difficult to see meaningful patterns. Therefore, I log transformed the economic indicators for better visualization.

Here are the distributions of the three economic indicators across all countries from 1960 to 2023.

  • Figure 1: This histogram illustrates the distribution of the log-transformed GDP across countries. The transformation helps normalize skewness from extremely large GDP values, making the distribution more symmetrical and suitable for modeling.
  • Figure 2: The unemployment rate distribution, after log transformation, shows a clearer view of variability across countries. Most countries fall within a relatively narrow range, though some outliers remain.
  • Figure 3: This plot shows the log-transformed inflation rate. The distribution reveals that while most countries have moderate inflation, a few experience significantly higher rates, even after transformation.

Here are the distributions of the five health indicators across all countries from 1960 to 2023.

  • Figure 4: This histogram shows the distribution of life expectancy across countries. Most countries have a life expectancy between 60 and 80 years, indicating relatively high health standards globally.
  • Figure 5: The mortality rate (under age 5) varies significantly. While many countries have low rates, a few exhibit extremely high mortality, reflecting healthcare disparities.
  • Figure 6: Immunization rates cluster around 80–100%, suggesting that many countries have well-established vaccination programs. However, the presence of lower values points to regions with less access.
  • Figure 7: Access to safe water is generally high, but a notable number of countries still fall below universal coverage, indicating room for infrastructure improvement.
  • Figure 8: Sanitation access shows a wider spread, with more countries lacking full coverage. This suggests that sanitation remains a key development challenge.
